# Mathematical model, v0.2

The default method changed in v0.2. The finite-palette v0.1 derivation is retained in `docs/legacy/math.md`. All coefficients below are synthetic. A recovered spectrum is a colorimetric prior, not an identified paint.

## Colorimetric convention

Inputs are bounded encoded sRGB. Decode the standard piecewise transfer function before all reconstruction. D65 times the CIE 1931 2° observer is integrated with trapezoidal endpoint weights over 380–780 nm. The reference grid is 5 nm (81 samples); the fast grid is 10 nm (41). Normalize XYZ so a perfect diffuser has Y=1; transform XYZ to linear sRGB using the matrix in `tools/model.py`. Finally normalize each RGB integration column so a perfect diffuser maps exactly to [1,1,1]. This small numerical white correction is part of the model, not an exact reproduction of unmodified standard tristimulus integration.

Write the resulting linear projection as A (3×N), and the normalized luminance quadrature as y (N). Public colors are encoded only after the optical calculation and gamut mapping.

## Independently fitted spectral anchors

Let ε=0.001, σ(z)=1/(1+exp(-z)), and D be the unscaled first-difference matrix on the 5 nm grid. For each linear RGB primary p, solve

    minimize_z ||A [ε + (1−2ε) σ(z)] − p||² + λ² ||Dz||²,
    λ=0.001.

There are no measured paint targets or external mixer outputs in this objective. Initialization solves bounded linear least squares in reflectance with a 0.01 first-difference penalty, then applies logit after clipping normalized reflectance to [0.0001,0.9999]. SciPy trust-region least squares uses an analytic Jacobian, 2,000 evaluation limit, and 1e−12 termination tolerances. Only the three primaries are optimized. Complement spectra are C=1−R, M=1−G, Y=1−B; W=1−ε and K=ε. This makes eight positive anchors, generated by three solves. First-difference logit smoothing is a prior on spectral shape, not a uniqueness or global-optimality claim.

## Continuous inversion without a LUT

For linear RGB x, sort the channels into l≤m≤h. Use weights l on W, 1−h on K, m−l on the secondary complementary to the minimum channel, and h−m on the maximum-channel primary. All other weights are zero. The weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Reconstruct

    r = Σ_i w_i B_i.

The recipe is piecewise affine and continuous. At a channel-order boundary the switching contribution has zero weight or the same secondary/primary limit. The first derivative need not be continuous. Bounds ε≤r≤1−ε hold for every input. The anchors are reconstruction functions; their weights are not asserted to be concentrations of physical pigments.

## Inferring separate K and S

Opaque Kubelka–Munk reflectance determines q=K/S, but not the absolute scale of either coefficient. We therefore declare the following synthetic optical prior:

    q_j = (1−r_j)² / (2r_j)
    Y = Σ_j y_j r_j
    s_n = 2Y / (1+Y²)
    η = 1 + (w_white−1) min(x),   w_white=2
    S_j = η sqrt[s_n/(1+q_j)]
    K_j = q_j S_j.

This is the β=1/2 member of S_j=η (1+q_j)^(−β) s_n^(1−β). β=0 assigns a luminance-normalized, wavelength-independent S; β=1 imposes K+S=η per band. Their geometric mean is a compromise between green mixtures and tint hue retention. The scalar η increases the optical strength of colors with a neutral white component. It leaves an isolated color's K/S ratio unchanged. Its value is an explicit artistic prior, not a titanium-white measurement.

K,S are nonnegative and S strictly positive. For a neutral reflectance r, s_n=1/(1+q), so K+S=η. No singular black or white appears because of the reflectance bounds.

## Residual and optical mixing

The latent state is z=(K,S,e), where e=x−Ar. For normalized nonnegative mixture weights α_i, combine all three components linearly:

    K_mix=Σα_i K_i; S_mix=Σα_i S_i; e_mix=Σα_i e_i.
    q_mix=K_mix/S_mix
    r_mix=1/[1+q_mix+sqrt(q_mix(q_mix+2))]
    x_mix=A r_mix+e_mix.

The rationalized reflectance avoids subtracting nearly equal numbers. The residual restores source color algebraically; float32 evaluation introduces small roundoff. Public pair mixing returns the exact supplied colors at t=0 or 1. Corrected reconstruction is an API consistency test, not a physical-quality measurement. The uncorrected error must also be reported.

Encoded optical states are closed under weighted mixing. Grouping states gives the same result up to floating-point rounding when group mass is carried into the outer weights. Decoding to RGB and re-encoding discards spectral state, so that operation is not associative. The large fast latent occupies 340 bytes, versus 44 bytes for the legacy concentration latent. Cache costs matter for a full canvas.

## Gamut and continuity

Retain the v0.1 continuous neutral-axis compression: let L=clamp(0.2126x_r+0.7152x_g+0.0722x_b,0,1), and choose the largest a∈[0,1] such that L+a(x−L) lies in the RGB cube. This is identity inside the gamut and preserves this luminance when it lies inside [0,1]. It does not preserve perceptual hue exactly.

The complete ideal-arithmetic map is continuous: simplex reconstruction, positive-coefficient optics, linear latent interpolation, stable reflectance inversion, gamut compression and the sRGB transfer function are continuous on the domain. This does not bound perceptual speed or guarantee monotone hue/chroma. The OKLab cube root is highly sensitive near black; black-to-white has a large first ΔEOK step at finite sampling even though the linear RGB curve converges continuously. Dense trajectories and endpoint-refinement tests document this distinction.

## Numerical separation

The reference uses the same model in f64 with 81 bands. The fast implementation uses f32 optical arrays at 41 bands, f64 sRGB transfer and final residual addition/gamut mapping. Both use stack arrays and the same source kernel. An independent NumPy implementation checks the complete f64 result, while a promoted-f64 decoder isolates float32 arithmetic on the fast state's already quantized coefficients. Spectral-resolution ablations hold the 5 nm fitted spectra fixed; the 1 nm control interpolates these spectra, rather than refitting them. Thus 5 nm is a computational reference, not continuum physical ground truth.
